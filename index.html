The Wall
The Wall
Individual Art Class Project
February – June, 2024
Wood, Strings, Arduino Board,
Motors, Acrylic Paint, Tools
Rhino, Enscape
36" × 48" × 3"
The development of technology encouraged more human communications without physical interactions, making face to face conversations harder and more precious. Inspired by the movement logic of plant stomata, the Wall is created to improve in person communications and social engagements. It opens and closes according to human emotions.
A divider between two bedrooms. The nets open when the wall detects happiness in the room, which means people are prone to communication. On the other hand, it closes when people are feeling negative, avoiding potentially bad interactions.
Stomata acts as the mouth of plants, A stoma can be influenced by many factors, such as the time of the day, humidity, and temperature of its surrounding. Just like plants, human can also be easily affected by those around them. This led to the question of whether the space people dwell in can also affect their emotions
Experimenting on web shapes: Fishnets with multiple layers. Strings open and close depending on elasticity.
Cutting out frames from plywood with jigsaw
Sketches exploring net movements
The algorithm has 6 phases, each representing a different emotion. Depending on the emotions that the camera detects, the code determines how much the wall will open.
The Wall installed in between two bedrooms of two siblings
A peak into the other sibling's room. The half open wall provides an opportunity for the siblings to connect.
Puzzle
Puzzle
Souvenir Based on the MIT Chapel
Group Class Project
May, 2026
Spray Paint, Embossed Paper,
Laser Cutting, Cast concrete
Varied Size
A souvenir for the MIT Chapel that explores shape evolution from 2D to 2.5D to 3D, using puzzle-like pieces to build a tactile connection between people, memory, and place.
Early studies translate the chapel's circular geometry and light conditions into modular puzzle forms.
The souvenir begins as a flat graphic system, testing how chapel-inspired shapes can interlock, separate, and recombine.
Form experiments move from planar patterns into raised surfaces, creating a 2.5D object that can be touched and assembled.
Iterations refine scale, edge condition, and the relationship between individual pieces and the complete chapel souvenir.
Stencil and fabrication drawings organize the graphic language before the final material tests.
Formwork studies prepare the concrete casting process, balancing handmade texture with repeatable geometry.
The final object combines cast concrete, embossed paper, laser-cut pieces, and painted surfaces into a compact chapel souvenir.
Presentation handouts explain the conceptual framework, fabrication process, and the souvenir's connection to the MIT Chapel.
The project communicates through diagrams, process documentation, and final product photography.
Additional presentation material documents the design logic and how each object belongs to the full puzzle system.
Material tests compare surface, color, embossing, and cast quality across multiple prototypes.
Wall studies connect the object back to the MIT Chapel interior, translating spatial atmosphere into a hand-held artifact.
The wall imagery records how light, texture, and curved surfaces informed the souvenir's material and graphic language.
Final wall documentation situates the puzzle pieces within the chapel reference system and closes the project narrative.
